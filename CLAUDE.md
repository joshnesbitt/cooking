# Cooking site

Josh's recipe site (Astro, deployed to Netlify). Posts live in `src/posts/`, one markdown file per recipe.

All prose on this site is published under Josh's name. It must read like he wrote it. The rules below are distilled from his personal style guide (`~/Obsidian/Everything/7_config/context/identity.md` and `writing.md`); if in doubt, those files are authoritative.

## Voice

- Direct, conversational, opinionated. Written for a peer, never for a search engine.
- UK English, Oxford comma. Always.
- Confident without being arrogant. Take positions plainly: "This is the only dressing you need", not "you might consider".
- Concrete over abstract. Name the ingredient, the brand, the dish, the restaurant. "Maldon" beats "flaky sea salt" when Maldon is what he uses.
- Honest about difficulty and failure. If a recipe is a faff or went wrong the first time, say so in a sentence, then move on.
- Cross-domain parallels are natural to him (cooking and engineering, restaurants and home kitchens). Use them when they genuinely help.
- Parenthetical asides with warmth are part of the voice: "(the good stuff)", "(don't skip this)".

## Recipe post shape

- Intro: one short paragraph anchored in something real. Where the recipe came from, why it exists, what prompted it. No throat-clearing, no "This recipe is a celebration of...".
- Sections: `## Ingredients`, `## Method`, `## Notes`. H2 only, rarely deeper.
- Method steps: imperative, one instruction per step, with the sensory cue that tells you it's working ("until golden-brown", "taste it on a leaf, not a spoon").
- Notes: the why behind the technique, make-ahead tips, and honest caveats. This is where opinion lives.
- Descriptions (frontmatter): plain and specific, like "Good oil, sharp acid, Maldon and pepper." Never marketing copy.

## Sentence rhythm

- Vary sentence length. Long flowing sentences broken by short punchy ones. Fragments are fine when they serve rhythm.
- Paragraphs of 2 to 5 sentences. Single-sentence paragraphs for emphasis.
- Vary sentence and paragraph openings. Never start consecutive paragraphs the same way.

## Anti-patterns (hard rules)

- No emoji. Ever.
- No em dashes. Use full stops, colons, commas, or parentheses.
- No comparative negation ("it's not X, it's Y"). State the positive directly.
- No forced groups of three. Two items or four are fine.
- No filler adverbs: "incredibly", "beautifully", "perfectly", "significantly". Use a specific detail or cut the word.
- No "-ing" trailing clauses that add fake depth ("...ensuring a perfect result", "...elevating the dish").
- No signposting ("let's dive in", "why this matters", "without further ado").
- No generic conclusions ("You won't regret it!", "Enjoy!"). Close with something specific or just stop.
- No title case in headings. Sentence case.
- No curly quotes. Straight quotes.
- Prefer "is/has" over "boasts/features/serves as/offers".

## Banned vocabulary

Never use: delve, deep dive (use "dig into"), elevate (as food-writing filler), leverage, utilise, transformative, game-changing, journey, unlock, unpack, double down, nuanced, tapestry, landscape (abstract), navigate (metaphor), ecosystem (unless literal), showcase, vibrant, rich (figurative), stunning, breathtaking, renowned, nestled, "in the heart of", sumptuous, decadent, indulgent, "take it to the next level", umami bomb, "restaurant-quality".

## The test

Before committing any prose, ask: "What makes this read like it came from a prompt?" If every sentence is the same length, if there are no opinions, if it could sit on any recipe site, rewrite it. Slightly rough edges and honest admissions are the voice. Authenticity over polish.
